The Coronado market

Coronado sits on a peninsula across the bay from downtown San Diego, reached by the San Diego-Coronado Bridge or by the Silver Strand from Imperial Beach. The Hotel del Coronado, opened in 1888, anchors the beachfront, and Naval Air Station North Island occupies the north end of the island. Orange Avenue serves as the main street through the Village. Strict zoning, a historic preservation program and limited land make it one of the most tightly held housing markets in the county.

Homes and housing stock

The Village holds Victorian, Craftsman and Spanish Revival homes alongside mid-century houses and recent custom rebuilds on narrow lots. Ocean Boulevard has large oceanfront homes. Coronado Shores is a group of high-rise condominium towers south of the Hotel del Coronado, and the Coronado Cays offer waterfront homes with private docks along channels near the Silver Strand. Smaller condominiums and duplexes are scattered through the Village.

What moves values in Coronado

Historic designation and Mills Act

Designated homes may carry Mills Act contracts that lower property taxes but restrict alterations.

North Island aircraft noise

Flight operations at the naval air station affect noise levels in parts of the Village.

Cays docks and water frontage

Dock size, channel width and boat access in the Coronado Cays can drive large value differences.

Shores condominium towers

High-rise units trade on floor level, view direction and building amenities, forming a separate market.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate and trust appraisals are common in Coronado because many families have held their homes for decades. Divorce appraisals often involve high-value Village homes or Cays waterfront properties. Property tax appeals and Mills Act-related valuations come up for historic homes, and pre-sale appraisals help owners set prices in a market with limited inventory. Military families also request values when receiving orders and deciding whether to sell or rent.
