Bostonia home appraisals

Unincorporated pocket north of El Cajon near Gillespie

Bostonia is an unincorporated area north and east of central El Cajon, bordering Gillespie Field and State Route 67. Its mix of older homes, mobile home parks and small subdivisions means comparable sales must be chosen by property type, not just location.

The Bostonia market

Bostonia sits between El Cajon, Santee and Lakeside, with Broadway, Pepper Drive and North Second Street as its main roads. The area grew in the early twentieth century and later filled in with postwar housing and small subdivisions. Its proximity to Gillespie Field, Interstate 8 and State Route 67 shapes both access and noise. Mailing addresses use El Cajon, which can obscure county jurisdiction in data searches, and parts of the area lie close to the city boundary.

Homes and housing stock

Housing includes 1940s through 1970s single-story ranch homes, some older cottages on larger lots and a number of mobile home parks. Small subdivisions and infill homes appear on what were once larger parcels. Lots range from standard sizes to deep parcels that may support ADUs or additional units under county rules, and detached garages and workshops are common on the older properties.

What moves values in Bostonia

Airport proximity

Homes near Gillespie Field may experience aircraft noise that buyers weigh, and local paired sales help measure its effect.

Mobile home parks

Mobile homes in Bostonia's parks require separate comparables and attention to space-rent terms.

Deep lots

Some older parcels are deep enough to support ADUs, which can add value if permitted.

County jurisdiction

County zoning and permits govern additions and units, so permit history matters in valuing improvements.

Appraisal assignments here

Probate and date-of-death appraisals are common in Bostonia because many homes have been in the same families for decades and heirs need a basis value. Bankruptcy and divorce work arises too, often involving modest houses or mobile homes where careful condition notes carry real weight. Properties with added units are a recurring challenge, and Brian Ward checks county permit records because permit status can change the result considerably.
