Lemon Grove home appraisals

Compact trolley town under its famous lemon

Lemon Grove is a small city wedged between southeastern San Diego, La Mesa and Spring Valley, with housing that changes noticeably from one block to the next. Comparable sales have to be drawn carefully because the closest sale across a city line may sit in a different market.

The Lemon Grove market

Incorporated in 1977, Lemon Grove covers under four square miles along State Route 94 and the Orange Line trolley. Its commercial center sits near Broadway and Lemon Grove Avenue, marked by the large lemon sculpture beside the trolley depot. Residential streets fan out north toward La Mesa and south toward Spring Valley, with hillier ground in Monterey Heights. The city's small size means many transactions draw comparables from neighboring jurisdictions, which requires attention to school districts and lot patterns.

Homes and housing stock

Most homes are single-story detached houses put up between the 1940s and the 1960s, frequently on lots larger than those in nearby city neighborhoods. Some older parcels retain semi-rural depth from the area's citrus-grove past, with long backyards and detached garages. Condominiums and small apartment buildings line Broadway and the arterials, and infill and ADU construction has increased as owners put deep lots to work.

What moves values in Lemon Grove

Deep lots and ADUs

Larger lots allow ADUs and lot splits, so appraisals must separate permitted from unpermitted units and value each correctly.

Highway 94 noise

Homes backing onto State Route 94 or the trolley line may need a location adjustment supported by local sales.

Cross-jurisdiction comparables

Sales from San Diego, La Mesa or Spring Valley need adjustments for differences in lot size and neighborhood appeal.

Hillside variation

Monterey Heights and other higher areas may carry views that are absent on flatter blocks a short distance away.

Appraisal assignments here

Lemon Grove sees many date-of-death and probate appraisals because of its long-tenured owner base and modest homes held for generations. Family transfers between parents and children are common, and Brian Ward is often asked to value properties with added units or garage conversions. Bankruptcy and divorce work also comes up, where documenting condition carefully matters as much as the sales data.
