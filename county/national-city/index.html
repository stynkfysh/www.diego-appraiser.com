National City home appraisals

Historic navy-and-auto city along the bay

National City is an older city with Victorian history, a naval presence and freeway-bounded neighborhoods. Values depend on condition, permits and proximity to industrial uses.

The National City market

National City incorporated in 1887, making it one of the oldest cities in the county, and grew up around the Kimball brothers' land holdings and the railroad. Today it combines older residential neighborhoods with the Mile of Cars auto dealerships, Westfield Plaza Bonita and the waterfront industrial land tied to Naval Base San Diego and the port. Interstate 5, Interstate 805 and State Route 54 divide the city into distinct sections. The Sweetwater River marks its southern border with Chula Vista.

Homes and housing stock

The city's housing ranges from Victorian and Craftsman homes in the older core, including the Brick Row rowhouses on A Avenue, to postwar bungalows and ranch houses on modest lots in El Toyon and Las Palmas. Apartments, condominiums and duplexes are common. Many detached homes have rear units, room additions or garage conversions, and some of that work was never permitted. Lincoln Acres is an unincorporated enclave of small single-family lots.

What moves values in National City

Industrial adjacency

Homes near port, shipyard or heavy commercial uses can trade at a discount to interior streets.

Freeway noise

Proximity to Interstate 5, Interstate 805 or State Route 54 affects both noise and air quality.

Unpermitted additions

Added rooms and garage conversions are common, and unpermitted space is typically treated differently from permitted living area.

Historic homes

Victorian and Craftsman houses draw a different buyer and need comparables of similar age and character.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate appraisals are common in National City, where many homes have been held by the same family for generations. Divorce appraisals frequently involve properties with second units or additions that need careful classification. Immigration sponsors often need a documented property value, and bankruptcy appraisals come up when owners must show equity to a trustee. Brian Ward also handles family-transfer appraisals between parents and children.
