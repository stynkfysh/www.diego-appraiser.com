Borrego Springs home appraisals

Dark-sky desert village inside Anza-Borrego

Borrego Springs is a desert village surrounded by Anza-Borrego Desert State Park, with golf communities, older homes and seasonal residents. Groundwater limits, seasonal demand and a small market make values here distinct from anywhere else in the county.

The Borrego Springs market

Borrego Springs sits in the Borrego Valley, reached by Montezuma Grade from the west or Yaqui Pass from the south. It is the only town completely ringed by Anza-Borrego, the biggest state park in California. The community was designated California's first International Dark Sky Community in 2009. Christmas Circle anchors the village, and metal sculptures by Ricardo Breceda at Galleta Meadows line the roads. Population swells in the cooler months with snowbirds and visitors.

Homes and housing stock

Housing ranges from mid-century desert homes and manufactured homes to golf-course properties in De Anza Country Club and Rams Hill. Lots vary from small village parcels to larger desert acreage. Many homes are single-story with block or frame construction, flat or low-pitched roofs and covered patios, and pools are common. Seasonal residents make up a large share of owners, and some homes sit vacant through the summer.

What moves values in Borrego Springs

Groundwater limits

The Borrego Valley groundwater basin is under a court-supervised adjudication that limits pumping. Water rights and availability can affect land and agricultural property values.

Seasonal market

Demand rises in winter and falls in summer. Brian Ward considers timing when analyzing comparable sales.

Golf course communities

Homes in De Anza and Rams Hill differ from village properties in amenities, HOA fees and buyer profile.

Desert construction

Heat, sun exposure and older cooling systems affect condition and effective age.

Appraisal assignments here

Many Borrego Springs owners are retirees or snowbirds, so estate and trust appraisals, often with a retrospective date of death, are a steady need. Divorce cases involving a desert second home, property tax assessment appeals and pre-sale valuations for seasonal properties also arise. Because sales cluster in the cooler months, Brian Ward pays close attention to the effective date and to which season the comparable sales closed in.
