Insurance Dispute Appraisals in San Diego

Before-and-after market value analysis for San Diego homeowners, adjusters and attorneys sorting out real estate losses from fire, water, flood, slope movement or other covered events.

After a serious loss, homeowners and insurers often disagree about more than repair costs. Questions can arise about how much the home's market value changed, what the property was worth just before the event, or whether lingering stigma affects value even after repairs. Brian Ward prepares residential appraisals that address these real estate value questions for policyholders, public adjusters, insurers and attorneys. The work does not replace a contractor's repair estimate or the policy's contents claim. Instead, it focuses on market value, using comparable sales and clear assumptions, so the parties can separate a measurable change in value from frustration or speculation.

What the appraisal can measure

Depending on the policy and the dispute, the report may estimate market value before the loss, value in damaged condition, value after repairs or a combination of those scenarios. It can also discuss whether a known event, such as a past landslide or major water intrusion, affects buyer behavior. The appraisal does not decide coverage or interpret the policy. It provides market evidence that the parties, an umpire or a court can weigh alongside contractor bids and other experts. Being precise about the question up front keeps the report focused.

Using the right date and condition

Insurance disputes often involve a value immediately before the event, which must be estimated retrospectively. Condition before the loss is reconstructed through prior photos, listing records, permits and owner documentation. A current value may also be needed if the home remains damaged or repairs are underway. Hypothetical conditions, such as valuing the home as if repairs were complete, are identified clearly so every reader understands which scenario the number represents. Clear labels prevent one value from being mistaken for another during negotiations or at a hearing.

How the work proceeds

The client explains the event, the date of loss and the specific question being argued. Brian Ward reviews available photos and reports, confirms the scope and provides a fee. An inspection documents the current condition, including damaged areas when safe access is possible. The report then compares relevant sales and explains how the loss or repair status was considered. Policyholders often share the report with a public adjuster or attorney, while insurers may use it in claim review. The scope stays limited to market value.

Complications to expect

Some losses affect only a portion of the home, while others change marketability for the entire neighborhood, such as a widely publicized flood. Repairs may still be incomplete, insurance funds may be delayed or the owner may have made upgrades during reconstruction. Comparable sales of damaged homes are scarce, so careful explanation is essential. When a policy's appraisal provision applies, the parties should follow its procedures and deadlines with legal guidance. The report notes these factors openly so readers can judge their weight.

In the City of San Diego

Insurance disputes within the City of San Diego can follow very different events. In January 2024, intense rainfall flooded homes in Southcrest, Shelltown, Encanto and Mountain View along the Chollas Creek watershed, leaving owners with water damage claims and questions about future marketability. Earlier wildfires reached city neighborhoods, including Scripps Ranch in 2003 and Rancho Bernardo in 2007. Coastal homes in La Jolla and Point Loma may face bluff erosion or slope concerns, while older canyon-rim homes in Mission Hills and Golden Hill can raise drainage and settlement questions. Each type of loss affects buyer perception differently.

Questions about insurance dispute appraisals

Can you determine the cost to repair my home?

No. Repair costs should come from qualified contractors, adjusters or construction estimators. Brian Ward analyzes real estate market value and how condition or a past event may affect what buyers pay. Those findings can be used alongside repair estimates, but they do not replace them. Combined, the two kinds of evidence give a fuller picture of the loss.

Can an appraisal show stigma after a repaired loss?

Sometimes, but stigma must be supported by market evidence rather than assumed. The report may examine sales of repaired homes, buyer reactions and the local reputation of the event. In many cases, fully repaired properties show little lasting effect, while others show measurable discounts. The analysis depends on the data available.

Can I hire you if my insurer has already done an appraisal?

Yes, as long as the engagement fits the policy process and there is no conflict. Brian Ward can prepare an independent report or review the existing one. If the dispute is in a formal policy appraisal process, check the policy requirements and your attorney's guidance before retaining anyone. A review can identify weaknesses in the existing report.
