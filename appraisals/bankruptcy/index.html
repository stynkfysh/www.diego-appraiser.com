Bankruptcy Appraisals in San Diego

Supported market values for San Diego homes and small rental buildings in Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 cases, written for debtors, attorneys and trustees reviewing scheduled equity.

Once a bankruptcy is filed, the number placed on the debtor's real estate affects almost everything that follows, from whether the home is protected by the homestead exemption to how much a Chapter 13 plan must pay unsecured creditors. Brian Ward appraises single family homes, condominiums and two to four unit properties for debtors and their counsel, as well as for trustees who want an independent check on a scheduled value. Reports are written for the bankruptcy setting, with a clear effective date tied to the petition or to a later hearing, and with the reasoning laid out so that a trustee or creditor can test it without guesswork.

How value drives the case

The scheduled value of real property, minus liens and costs of sale, determines how much equity exists beyond the exemption. In a Chapter 7, meaningful unprotected equity can prompt the trustee to consider selling the home. In a Chapter 13, the same equity often sets the minimum the plan must return to unsecured creditors. Lien stripping of a wholly unsecured second mortgage also depends on showing that the home is worth less than the first loan balance. In each situation, a credible appraisal is the piece of evidence everyone looks at first.

Petition date and other effective dates

Most bankruptcy appraisals are effective as of the petition filing date, since that is when the estate is created and exemptions are measured. Some matters call for a later date, such as a confirmation hearing, a motion to value collateral, or a trustee's proposed sale. Brian Ward confirms the date with counsel at the outset. When the filing is recent, the analysis uses sales that closed around the petition; when an older petition is involved, the report is written as a retrospective valuation.

What the process looks like

Counsel or the debtor sends the property address, the case chapter, the petition date and the purpose of the report. Brian Ward provides a fee and turnaround. An inspection is arranged with the debtor, and the visit documents condition honestly, including repairs the home needs, since deferred maintenance affects value in a bankruptcy just as it would in any sale. The finished report is delivered as a PDF suitable for attaching to schedules, motions or trustee correspondence. Turnaround expectations are set at the start so the report arrives before any filing or hearing deadline.

Challenges that come up

Trustees sometimes obtain a broker price opinion or an automated estimate that comes in higher than the debtor's figure, and the appraisal must explain why its comparables are a better match. Condition is often the key difference, since automated tools assume average upkeep. Other complications include unpermitted units, co-owners who are not in the bankruptcy, a home held in a trust, or a pending sale. If a value is contested at a hearing, Brian Ward can testify about how the opinion was formed.

In the City of San Diego

Bankruptcy cases for San Diego County are filed in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of California, located at the Jacob Weinberger courthouse on West F Street downtown. California's homestead exemption was raised in 2021 and is now tied to the county median sale price within a floor and ceiling, which in San Diego means the protected amount is substantial, but equity in long-held homes in neighborhoods like Bay Park, Del Cerro or Allied Gardens can still exceed it. Condominiums in Mission Valley and Hillcrest raise HOA delinquency and special assessment issues that a trustee will want addressed.

What to have ready

  • The petition date, or the planned filing date
  • The case number and chapter, if already filed
  • Current mortgage and lien balances
  • Photos or a list of significant repair needs
  • Any trustee or creditor valuation already received
  • Contact information for access to the home

Questions about bankruptcy appraisals

Should I get the appraisal before or after filing?

Many attorneys order the report shortly before filing so the schedules reflect a supported value from the start. If the case is already filed, a report effective as of the petition date can still be prepared. Your attorney will know which approach suits your chapter and timeline. Either way, the effective date is stated clearly in the report.

The trustee's number is higher than mine. What happens next?

A differing value often prompts a conversation rather than a hearing. A well-documented appraisal that explains condition, location and comparable selection gives your attorney something concrete to present. If the disagreement continues, the court may hear evidence, and Brian Ward can testify about his report when needed. Sometimes a short written response to the trustee resolves the gap.

Can you appraise only my share of a jointly owned home?

The appraisal normally values the whole property, and the attorneys then apply ownership percentages. Fractional interest discounts are a separate and more specialized question. Brian Ward will discuss with your counsel what the case needs before quoting, so the report answers the right question. Co-owner details also help confirm who should receive the report.
