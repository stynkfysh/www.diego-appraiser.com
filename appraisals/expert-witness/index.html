Expert Witness & Litigation Support in San Diego

Real estate valuation opinions, report reviews and courtroom testimony from a California certified residential appraiser, for San Diego attorneys handling property-related disputes and lawsuits.

When a case turns on what a home was worth, the person who formed that opinion may need to explain it under oath. Brian Ward provides residential appraisals prepared with litigation in mind, review of opposing appraisals, and deposition and trial testimony. Matters range from partition actions and partnership breakups to construction defect, disclosure and probate disputes. Since 2004 he has completed more than 7,000 appraisals, and that volume of fieldwork is what gives an opinion depth when it is cross-examined. Attorneys receive a report built to be read by a judge or jury, with reasoning a non-specialist can follow and assumptions stated openly.

Services available to counsel

Litigation support usually begins with an appraisal of the subject property as of the date relevant to the claim. It may also include a review of another appraiser's report, identifying where the comparable selection, adjustments or assumptions fall short. Some attorneys ask for a consulting role first, to understand whether a valuation issue is worth pursuing before any report is disclosed. When the matter proceeds, testimony at deposition, arbitration or trial explains the work and responds to the other side's expert.

Retrospective and hypothetical scenarios

Litigation often asks about the past or about conditions that did not exist. A disclosure case may need the value of a home with and without an undisclosed defect, measured on the date of purchase. A construction case may compare value as built against value as promised. Brian Ward uses extraordinary assumptions and hypothetical conditions only where appropriate and labels them clearly, because opposing counsel will look for any assumption that was not disclosed. Each effective date is tied to the evidence available at that time.

How engagements are structured

An engagement typically starts with a conflict check and a conversation with counsel about the issues, dates and deadlines. Brian Ward then quotes the appraisal and sets an hourly rate for review, consultation and testimony. The report follows USPAP, the professional standards that govern appraisers, and work product protections are managed by counsel. Scheduling for depositions and trial is coordinated well ahead whenever possible, since court calendars often shift with little notice. Engagement terms are put in writing before work begins.

What makes valuation testimony hold up

Most challenges target comparable selection, adjustment support and condition. A report that explains why each sale was chosen, why others were rejected and how adjustments were derived gives cross-examination less to work with. Brian Ward keeps notes and data organized so questions can be answered directly. He testifies to his own opinion and its basis, not to legal conclusions, and he does not tailor values to the retaining party's preference, which is ultimately what makes an expert credible to a trier of fact.

In the City of San Diego

Property disputes in the City of San Diego are typically heard at San Diego Superior Court downtown, with the Hall of Justice handling many civil matters and the Central Courthouse hearing family law. Local valuation issues that show up in litigation include coastal bluff erosion in La Jolla and Point Loma, Proposition D's 30 foot height limit west of Interstate 5 affecting redevelopment potential, short-term rental licensing under the city's tiered ordinance, and canyon-edge lots in neighborhoods like Mission Hills and Golden Hill where slope stability can be contested. Partition actions among siblings who inherited a home are also common.

Questions about expert witness & litigation support

Can you review the other side's appraisal without doing your own?

Yes. A review examines whether another appraiser's report is credible, well supported and compliant with professional standards. It can identify weak comparables, unsupported adjustments or factual errors. A review does not by itself produce a new value unless that is added to the scope, which some attorneys request when the other report is badly flawed.

How is testimony billed?

Deposition and trial testimony, preparation and travel are billed at an hourly rate provided before the engagement. Many attorneys also pay a retainer for testimony. The appraisal or review itself is quoted separately. Brian Ward will discuss rates and cancellation terms up front so there are no surprises when a court date moves.

How early should we retain an appraiser?

Earlier is better. Bringing the appraiser in before discovery closes allows time to inspect the property, gather historical data and shape the report to address the issues in the case. Waiting until shortly before expert disclosure deadlines can limit options, especially when a retrospective date or a property access issue is involved.
