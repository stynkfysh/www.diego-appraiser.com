Pre-Purchase Appraisals in San Diego

An independent value opinion for San Diego buyers who want to know what a home is really worth before they write an offer or remove their appraisal contingency.

Buyers often discover too late that the price they agreed to was driven more by competition than by evidence. A pre-purchase appraisal gives a buyer an independent opinion of value before the commitment becomes final. Brian Ward works for the buyer alone, not the lender, the seller or either agent, and the report is written to help a person make a decision rather than to clear an underwriting checklist. It is useful for cash purchases, private or off-market deals, homes with unusual features that make pricing hard, and any purchase where the buyer simply wants a second opinion grounded in the market before signing.

What the buyer learns

The report gives an opinion of market value and, more importantly, the reasoning behind it. Buyers see which recent sales were most comparable, how differences in size, condition, view and lot were weighed, and where the asking price sits relative to that evidence. That context helps a buyer decide whether to offer full price, negotiate, cover a potential appraisal gap or walk away. It also flags issues, such as unpermitted space or an awkward floor plan, that could affect resale later.

Timing within the escrow

A pre-purchase appraisal works best before the offer or during the contingency period. California purchase contracts typically include appraisal and investigation contingencies with deadlines measured in days, so Brian Ward schedules quickly when a buyer is in escrow. If the property has not yet been offered on, the buyer may request a desktop report based on listing photos and records, then upgrade to an inspection later if the purchase moves forward. The effective date is always current. Buyers who are still shopping can use that early read across several homes.

Getting access and moving fast

Because the seller controls the home, the buyer's agent usually arranges the inspection, often alongside a home inspection or a second showing. Brian Ward needs the address, the contract or list price and the contingency deadline. A fee and turnaround are confirmed before scheduling. The finished report is sent directly to the buyer, who decides whether to share it with an agent or a lender, since this is a private appraisal rather than one ordered for a mortgage. The buyer controls who sees it.

Where pre-purchase opinions matter most

Homes with a single standout feature, such as a panoramic view, an oversized canyon lot or a detached ADU, are hard to price from sales alone and benefit most. So do properties that have been flipped, where finishes may look new while the underlying systems are not. Off-market and family-to-family sales lack the price discovery of open bidding. In each case, an appraiser who is not paid on commission and not tied to a loan can offer a clearer view.

In the City of San Diego

Buyers in the City of San Diego encounter very different pricing logic from one neighborhood to the next. A canyon-view lot in South Park, a condo in a newer Little Italy tower, a ranch house in Del Cerro and a beach-close cottage in Pacific Beach each depend on features that automated estimates handle poorly. Properties in Mission Beach and Pacific Beach can carry short-term rental history that may not continue under the city's licensing caps. Homes inside the Coastal Overlay Zone or in a designated historic district have added permitting steps, and buyers planning a remodel or ADU should understand those limits before settling on a price.

Questions about pre-purchase appraisals

Can a lender use my pre-purchase appraisal?

Usually not. a lender has to order its appraisal through its own ordering system under federal independence rules, so a private report typically cannot replace the lender's appraisal. A pre-purchase report is for your decision making. It can still help when your lender's appraisal comes in low, because it shows how a second professional read the same market.

How quickly can this be done during escrow?

Turnaround depends on the schedule and the property, but Brian Ward prioritizes contingency deadlines when possible. Tell him the date your contingency expires when you first reach out, and he will confirm whether that timeline is realistic before you commit. A desktop report can sometimes be completed faster if an inspection is not essential.

Will the appraisal tell me whether to buy the house?

No. The appraisal gives an independent opinion of market value and explains the evidence behind it. Whether to buy, how much to offer and how much risk to accept remain your decisions. Many buyers find the report helps them think through those choices with their agent or lender more confidently.
