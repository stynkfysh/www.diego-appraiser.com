Immigration Appraisals in San Diego

Documented market values for San Diego real estate used as financial evidence in immigration matters, including sponsor asset declarations and proof of funds requirements.

Some immigration filings ask a sponsor, applicant or investor to show financial resources, and real estate equity can be part of that picture. When it is, a simple online estimate rarely carries much weight. Brian Ward provides residential appraisals that document the current market value of a San Diego home, condominium or small rental property so it can be paired with mortgage statements to show available equity. The report is prepared independently for the client and their immigration attorney, with a clear effective date, property description and comparable sales. Brian Ward does not give immigration advice; counsel decides whether and how the report is submitted.

Where property value enters a filing

On Form I-864, the Affidavit of Support, a sponsor whose income falls short may use assets to make up the difference, and USCIS instructions allow real estate equity to be counted when it is properly documented. The rules generally require assets to be convertible to cash within one year and set the needed amount as a multiple of the income shortfall. Other filings that request proof of financial ability may also accept property value as evidence. The appraisal supplies the market value part of that calculation.

What the report documents

The appraisal identifies the property, its ownership as provided by the client, its physical characteristics and an opinion of current market value. It explains which sales were used and why. The report does not calculate equity, since that depends on loan balances, liens and the attorney's interpretation of the applicable rules. Instead, it gives the immigration file a professionally supported value that can be combined with mortgage statements, title records and proof of ownership. Any assumptions about access or condition are stated plainly in the report.

Timing and process

Immigration packages often have firm deadlines, so the client should share the filing timeline when requesting a quote. Brian Ward confirms the property, owner and intended users, then schedules an inspection or recommends a desktop approach when appropriate. The report is delivered as a PDF that can be included with other supporting documents. If an officer later requests updated evidence, a new report with a later effective date may be needed. Brian Ward quotes a fee and delivery date before work begins, so the client can plan around the filing date.

Common questions from sponsors

Sponsors sometimes assume that the full property value counts, when the relevant figure is usually equity after debts. Others own the home jointly with someone who is not part of the filing, or hold title through a trust. Recent purchase prices may not reflect current value. These details should be reviewed with an immigration attorney, while the appraisal focuses on reporting the property's market value accurately and transparently. Sharing those details at the outset helps Brian Ward define the right scope and avoids a report that misses what the attorney needs.

In the City of San Diego

San Diego's position on the border means many residents of the City of San Diego support relatives through the immigration process. Homes used as sponsor assets may be anywhere from San Ysidro and Otay Mesa to Mira Mesa, Linda Vista or Rancho Bernardo. Values can differ significantly within these communities, depending on lot size, updates, ADU status and proximity to commercial corridors or freeways. Some owners hold title with relatives or have unpermitted additions, which can complicate the evidence. A local appraisal explains the specific features of the property rather than relying on a broad citywide average that may overstate or understate equity.

Questions about immigration appraisals

Will USCIS accept an appraisal as evidence of property value?

USCIS instructions for asset documentation have accepted evidence of real estate value, including appraisals by licensed appraisers, but each case depends on the form and the officer's review. Your immigration attorney should confirm what documents to submit. Brian Ward's role is to provide a credible, independent value opinion. Requirements can change, so current instructions should always be checked.

Can my home equity count as an asset?

That is a legal question for your immigration attorney, because it depends on the form, the sponsor's circumstances and current instructions. Generally, the relevant figure is net equity after mortgages and liens, not the full market value. The appraisal provides the value needed to perform that calculation. Your attorney then decides how it fits the filing.

How current does the appraisal need to be?

Agencies usually want evidence that reflects current circumstances. A report dated close to the filing is generally more persuasive than an older one. If your case is delayed or the market changes, your attorney may recommend an update. Brian Ward can prepare a new report with a later effective date when needed.
