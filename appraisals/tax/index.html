Tax Appraisals in San Diego

Appraisals for property tax appeals, gift tax filings, charitable donations and capital gains basis questions, covering San Diego homes, condos and small rental buildings.

Tax questions about real estate usually come down to one figure: what the property was worth on a particular date. Brian Ward prepares appraisals that support decline-in-value requests and assessment appeals with the San Diego County Assessor, gift tax returns when a home or interest is transferred to family, charitable contribution deductions and basis determinations. Each assignment begins by identifying which agency or advisor will read the report, because the Assessment Appeals Board, the IRS and a CPA each look for different things. The report then supplies a value with its supporting evidence, written in plain language and tied to the effective date the rules require.

Property tax appeals and decline-in-value reviews

California's Proposition 13 sets a base year value that rises by no more than two percent a year. When market value falls below that factored base, owners can ask the Assessor for a temporary reduction, often called a Prop 8 review, and can appeal to the Assessment Appeals Board if the request is denied. The relevant date is January 1 of the tax year in question. An appraisal effective on that lien date, using sales close to it, is the clearest evidence an owner can present.

Gift, estate and basis work

When a parent deeds a home or a share of one to a child, the IRS expects a gift tax return reporting fair market value on the transfer date. Donating real estate to a charity triggers federal rules that set standards for both the report and the person who signs it. Basis questions arise when an owner sells and needs to reconstruct value at a past date, such as the date a property converted from a residence to a rental. Brian Ward confirms the date and intended user before starting each one.

How a tax assignment proceeds

The client or CPA provides the address, the tax purpose and the effective date. Brian Ward reviews whether an inspection adds meaningful accuracy, then quotes a fee and delivery date. For an appeal, the filing deadline shapes the timeline, so earlier contact helps. After research and analysis, the report is delivered as a PDF that can be uploaded with the appeal application, attached to a gift return or kept in the tax file for future reference. Copies remain available if the Assessor or IRS asks questions later.

Pitfalls to avoid

The most common mistake is using the wrong date, such as a current value for an appeal that concerns a prior lien date. Another is relying on an online estimate that misses condition or lot issues. Partial interests may involve discounts that a residential appraiser should not apply without proper scope, so those cases are discussed with the CPA first. Appeals can also be time-sensitive, and some hearings come months after filing, which means the appraisal must stand on its own without the appraiser present.

In the City of San Diego

Property taxes in the City of San Diego are administered by the San Diego County Assessor, with appeals heard by the county's Assessment Appeals Board. Long-held homes in neighborhoods like Clairemont, Serra Mesa and Linda Vista often carry low Proposition 13 bases, while recent buyers in Carmel Valley or downtown high-rises can see assessments that lag behind a cooling market. Proposition 19 narrowed the parent-child exclusion that Proposition 58 once provided, so transfers on or after February 16, 2021 often need a value to show how much of the assessment is excluded. Mills Act properties in historic districts are valued under a separate income-based formula by the Assessor.

Questions about tax appraisals

Is an appraisal required to file a property tax appeal?

No, but it is usually the strongest evidence an owner can submit. The Appeals Board weighs sales data close to the January 1 lien date. A report from a certified appraiser organizes that evidence and explains it, which matters because hearing officers see many appeals supported only by online estimates or unadjusted sale lists.

My parents want to deed me their house. Why would we need an appraisal?

A gift of real estate is generally reported on a federal gift tax return at fair market value, and Proposition 19 may also require the Assessor to compare market value with the existing assessed value. A credible appraisal on the transfer date supports both. Your CPA or attorney can confirm what your specific situation requires.

Can you value my home for a past tax year?

Yes. Retrospective appraisals for prior lien dates or transfer dates are routine. The report uses sales that closed around the chosen date and describes the property as it existed then. Whether a past year can still be appealed depends on filing deadlines, which your tax advisor or the Assessor's office can confirm.
