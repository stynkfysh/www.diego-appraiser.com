Bay Terraces home appraisals

Late-1970s tract homes along Paradise Valley

Bay Terraces is the largest neighborhood in the Skyline-Paradise Hills planning area, made up mostly of tract homes built in the late 1970s and early 1980s. Its newer housing stock and HOA communities set it apart from older surrounding neighborhoods.

The Bay Terraces market

Bay Terraces lies south of Skyline and east of Paradise Hills, stretching toward State Route 54 and Bonita. Paradise Valley Road divides it into North Bay Terrace and South Bay Terrace. Most of the neighborhood was built in the late 1970s and early 1980s on former canyon land, and some sections sit within planned developments with HOAs. A large Filipino-American community settled here, drawn by affordable homes near the Navy bases. Bell Middle School, Penn Elementary and O'Farrell Park serve the area. Because its homes are newer than those in neighboring areas, sales from Skyline or Paradise Hills need age adjustments. Brian Ward weighs HOA status, home age, lot size and street location carefully when building a comparable set.

How Bay Terraces took shape

San Diego annexed the Bay Terraces area in 1969. Development plans revised in the mid-1970s led to construction of tract homes in the late 1970s and early 1980s. Many homes were priced beyond the reach of local residents at first, and some sections stood partially built for years. Federal programs helped military families and others buy homes. Filipino Navy families settled here in large numbers through the 1990s. In 2015, a bridge on the neighborhood's southern edge was named for labor leaders Larry Itliong and Philip Vera Cruz.

Homes and housing stock

Most homes are tract houses from the late 1970s and early 1980s, in both single-story and two-story plans, typically between 1,200 and 2,000 square feet with attached two-car garages. Some tracts are governed by HOAs with common areas and monthly dues. Lots are moderate, with some on slopes above Paradise Valley. Condominiums and townhomes form a separate segment, and the large Meadowbrook Apartments complex provides a block of rental housing. Because many homes came from the same builders, floor plans repeat, which can make matched-plan comparisons useful. Window bars, wrought-iron gates and chain-link fencing are common.

Appraisal assignments here

Original buyers from the late 1970s and early 1980s are now in their later years, which brings trust and date-of-death appraisals and parent-to-child transfers. Divorce appraisals often involve HOA homes where dues and condition matter. Because many owners are Navy veterans, appraisals sometimes support estate planning or relocation decisions. Pre-sale reports help families decide whether to sell or rent out a home after a move.

Bay Terraces appraisal questions

Are Bay Terraces homes compared with Skyline homes?

Sometimes, but Bay Terraces homes are generally newer than Skyline's 1950s and 1960s tracts. When nearby sales from Skyline or Paradise Hills are used, Brian Ward adjusts for age, plan design and lot differences, and prefers same-tract sales because repeated floor plans make for the most direct comparisons.

Do HOA dues affect value?

They can. Monthly dues add to ownership cost, while common-area amenities may offset that for some buyers. Brian Ward considers dues and amenities when comparing HOA and non-HOA homes, and prefers comparables from the same or similar associations when they are available.
