College Area home appraisals

Mesa neighborhoods around San Diego State University

The College Area wraps around San Diego State University, up on the mesa beyond Talmadge and north of Rolando. Student demand, hillside homes in Alvarado Estates and rooming-house rules make this a market where nearby sales can mean very different things.

The College Area market

San Diego State University shapes the College Area. Student rentals, faculty homes and new campus-area apartments sit near quieter residential streets. The SDSU Transit Center connects the campus to the Green Line trolley, and College Avenue, Montezuma Road and El Cajon Boulevard carry most of the traffic. Alvarado Estates and College View Estates offer bigger homes on hillside and canyon lots, while blocks closer to campus have many single-family homes converted to student rentals. Newer mid-rise apartments and mixed-use buildings line parts of College Avenue and El Cajon Boulevard. For appraisers, the main challenge is separating homes valued by owner-occupants from those valued for rental income, and understanding how city rules on rooming houses and parking affect demand.

How College Area took shape

San Diego State moved from its Park Boulevard campus to Montezuma Mesa in 1931, and the College Area grew around it. Postwar tract development filled much of the mesa in the late 1940s and 1950s, and hillside neighborhoods like Alvarado Estates were built with larger homes. Student enrollment growth led to more rentals and later to city rules on mini-dorms. Campus expansion and redevelopment along College Avenue and El Cajon Boulevard have added apartments and student housing.

Homes and housing stock

Most single-family homes are postwar ranch houses from the late 1940s through the 1960s, often 1,200 to 2,000 square feet on lots of 6,000 square feet or more. Alvarado Estates and College View Estates offer bigger custom homes on canyon or hillside lots, some with pools and views. Many homes near campus were expanded with added bedrooms and bathrooms for student rentals. Apartment buildings, newer student housing and some condos are common near College Avenue and El Cajon Boulevard. Lot sizes are generally larger than in the older central neighborhoods.

What moves values in College Area

Student rental demand

Homes near campus may sell to investors based on rental income. Comparables are separated by owner-occupant and investor demand.

Rooming-house rules

City rules limit rooming houses and occupancy, affecting rental potential. Brian Ward considers how these rules affect demand and value.

Added bedrooms

Many homes have extra bedrooms added for students, some without permits. Permit history and functional layout are reviewed.

Hillside homes

Alvarado Estates and other hillside areas have larger lots and views but may have fire or slope issues. These are adjusted separately from flat tract homes.

Traffic and campus activity

Homes near College Avenue or Montezuma Road get more traffic and noise. Paired sales measure any difference.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate and trust appraisals often involve postwar homes owned by retired faculty or long-time residents. Divorce cases may include rental homes near campus whose income is part of the settlement. Pre-sale appraisals help owners price homes with added bedrooms, where permit status and layout can affect demand. Family transfers are common when parents buy homes for students and later sell or transfer them, which raises gift-tax and basis questions.

College Area appraisal questions

How are student-rental homes valued?

Brian Ward considers both comparable sales and rental income when the likely buyers are investors. He reviews permitted bedrooms, layout and city occupancy rules, because those affect what a buyer can legally rent, and he does not count unpermitted bedrooms as legal living space.

Are Alvarado Estates homes compared with other College Area homes?

Only with adjustments. Alvarado Estates homes are often larger, on hillside lots with views and privacy, and they draw different buyers than tract homes near campus. The best comparables are other Alvarado Estates sales or similar hillside homes nearby, with tract sales used only to support adjustments.
