Loma Portal home appraisals

Spanish Revival streets under the airport flight path

Loma Portal sits on the north slope of Point Loma, close to downtown and Liberty Station but directly beneath departing flights from San Diego International Airport. Aircraft noise, view and housing era all have to be weighed when comparing homes here.

The Loma Portal market

Loma Portal climbs from the flats along Rosecrans Street and Midway Drive toward the Point Loma ridge, west of the airport and north of the Point Loma Heights area. Its streets hold many 1920s through 1940s Spanish Revival and Tudor homes, and the higher blocks get views of the bay, downtown and Mission Bay. Liberty Station, the former Naval Training Center, borders the neighborhood to the east with shops, schools, parks and newer homes. Jets departing to the west pass low over much of Loma Portal, and noise levels vary noticeably by street. Many homes have been sound-insulated through the airport's residential noise program. Brian Ward compares homes within the same noise and view setting where possible, because buyers often treat a quieter view street very differently from a flat block under the flight path.

How Loma Portal took shape

Loma Portal was subdivided in the 1910s and 1920s as a residential tract between the bay and the Point Loma hills. The Naval Training Center opened next door in 1923, and Lindbergh Field began service in 1928, shaping the neighborhood for decades. Many homes were built during the 1920s and 1930s in revival styles. After the Naval Training Center closed in 1997, the City and developer McMillin redeveloped the site as Liberty Station, adding new detached homes and townhomes on the neighborhood's eastern edge.

Homes and housing stock

The core of Loma Portal is detached single-family homes from the 1920s through 1950s, including Spanish Revival, Tudor and Monterey styles, with some postwar ranch houses on higher streets. Lots are typically moderate in size, and many homes are between about 1,200 and 2,500 square feet. Liberty Station adds homes and townhomes built in the 2000s with HOA dues. Apartments and older condos cluster near Rosecrans Street and the Midway area. Homes that participated in the airport's sound insulation program may have upgraded windows and doors, and some carry recorded avigation easements.

What moves values in Loma Portal

Airport noise contour

Location within the higher noise contours affects buyer demand. Comparables are chosen from similar flight-path positions where possible.

Sound insulation and avigation easements

Homes treated through the airport noise program often have an avigation easement recorded on title. The appraisal notes it and considers whether the market treats it differently.

View elevation

Upper streets with bay and downtown views trade well above flat blocks near Rosecrans Street.

Period architecture

Original revival-era homes with intact character can draw a premium, and some may qualify for historic designation or Mills Act contracts.

New construction at Liberty Station

Newer HOA homes at Liberty Station compete with older detached homes but differ in age, lot size and dues, so they are adjusted carefully.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate and trust appraisals are common for older revival homes held by original families. Divorce appraisals often require comparing a home under the flight path with sales on quieter streets. Property tax appeals arise when aircraft noise was not reflected in an assessed value. Pre-sale and pre-purchase reports help buyers and sellers understand how noise and view interact in this specific market.

Loma Portal appraisal questions

How does airport noise affect Loma Portal home values?

The effect varies by street, elevation and how the home is built. Brian Ward reviews where the property and comparables sit relative to the departure path, notes any sound insulation, and relies on local sales to support adjustments rather than applying a fixed percentage.

Are Liberty Station homes good comparables for older Loma Portal houses?

They can be used with care. Liberty Station homes are newer, sit in an HOA and often on smaller lots, while older Loma Portal homes may have more land and character. When better matches exist, they are preferred, and when Liberty Station sales are used, age, dues and lot differences are adjusted.
