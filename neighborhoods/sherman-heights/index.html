Victorian historic district minutes from downtown

Sherman Heights holds one of San Diego's largest concentrations of Victorian homes, many within a designated historic district just east of downtown. Historic status, condition and unit count all shape value on these older blocks.

The Sherman Heights market

Sherman Heights sits just east of downtown's East Village and south of Golden Hill, with Logan Heights below it to the south. Its blocks are lined with Victorian, Queen Anne and Craftsman homes, many restored by owners attracted to the historic character and proximity to downtown. The Villa Montezuma, an ornate 1887 mansion, stands as a museum. Much of the neighborhood is within the Sherman Heights Historic District, which regulates exterior changes. Some properties have Mills Act contracts that reduce property taxes in exchange for preservation. The neighborhood also has many rentals, small apartment buildings and homes divided into units. Brian Ward reviews historic status, tax contracts and permit history carefully because each affects what a buyer will pay.

How Sherman Heights took shape

Matthew Sherman bought land here in 1867 and subdivided it as Sherman's Addition, one of San Diego's earliest residential areas outside the original town. During the 1880s land boom, wealthy residents built ornate Victorian homes, including the Villa Montezuma. The neighborhood declined in the mid-1900s as wealthier residents moved to newer suburbs and freeways cut nearby areas. Mexican-American families became a large share of residents. Preservation efforts in the late 1900s led to the historic district designation and renewed interest in restoration.

Homes and housing stock

Housing is dominated by Victorian, Queen Anne, Stick and Craftsman homes from the 1880s through 1920s, along with some Spanish Revival and postwar houses. Many large Victorians have been converted to multiple units or have rear cottages behind them. Lots are typical urban parcels on a regular grid. Some homes are fully restored, while others need significant structural and system work. Small apartment buildings and newer infill also appear. Homes range widely in size, from small cottages to large Victorians over 2,500 square feet. Historic district rules affect exterior alterations, and Mills Act contracts reduce taxes on some properties.

What moves values in Sherman Heights

Historic district rules

Exterior changes require review, which affects renovation costs and options.

Mills Act contracts

Reduced property taxes can increase value for buyers, but contracts carry preservation obligations.

Restoration quality

Fully restored homes trade at a premium over those needing work, so condition adjustments must be supported.

Unit conversions

Many Victorians have been divided into units. Permit status determines value contribution.

Downtown proximity

Walking distance to downtown and East Village supports demand, though freeway noise affects some blocks.

Appraisal assignments here

Historic status puts property tax questions near the top of the list here, including how a Mills Act contract affects value for assessment or sale. Date-of-death and trust appraisals are frequent because many homes have been in families for decades. Divorce appraisals often involve restored Victorians where the quality of restoration is disputed, or houses split into units. Pre-sale reports help heirs understand what restoration-minded buyers will pay for a house needing work.

Sherman Heights appraisal questions

Does a Mills Act contract increase a Sherman Heights home's value?

It can, because reduced property taxes lower the cost of ownership, but the contract also carries preservation obligations that transfer to the buyer. Brian Ward considers whether comparable sales had similar contracts and how buyers have treated them, and notes the contract and its obligations in the report.

How is a Victorian divided into units valued?

The appraisal confirms legal units through permit records, then compares the property with similar multi-unit Victorians or adjusts sales of single-family homes for the added units. Unpermitted units receive limited credit. Historic district rules can also affect how easily units are legalized, and the treatment is explained clearly in the report.
