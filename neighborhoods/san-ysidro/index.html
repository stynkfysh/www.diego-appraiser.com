San Ysidro home appraisals

Little Landers colony turned gateway at the border

San Ysidro sits beside the San Ysidro Port of Entry, the hemisphere's busiest land port of entry. Small historic lots, multi-unit properties and newer townhomes share the same few square miles, so the right comparables are rarely the closest ones.

The San Ysidro market

San Ysidro is bordered by Otay Mesa to the east, Nestor and the Tijuana River Valley to the west, and Mexico to the south. San Ysidro Boulevard is the historic main street, lined with exchange houses, clinics and family businesses, while Camino de la Plaza and Las Americas Premium Outlets serve cross-border shoppers. The Blue Line trolley ends at the San Ysidro station beside the pedestrian crossing, with another stop at Beyer Boulevard. Interstate 5, Interstate 805 and State Route 905 converge on the community. Housing includes older bungalows, ranch homes, duplexes, apartments and newer townhomes. Many households work, study or have family on both sides of the border, which keeps demand steady for homes close to the crossing despite traffic and noise.

How San Ysidro took shape

In 1909, William E. Smythe founded the Little Landers colony here on the idea that a family could live off a small, intensively farmed plot, and the original small lots still shape the street grid. The settlement grew into a border town as crossings increased through the twentieth century. San Diego annexed San Ysidro in 1957. The port of entry was rebuilt and expanded in phases during the 2010s, including the PedWest crossing, while retail along Camino de la Plaza grew around cross-border shoppers.

Homes and housing stock

Older homes include small bungalows and cottages on the original Little Landers lots, along with 1950s and 1960s ranch houses. Typical detached homes run 900 to 1,500 square feet. Duplexes, triplexes and small apartment buildings are common, and many single-family lots hold a second unit, permitted or not. Newer townhome and condominium projects have been added on infill sites near the freeways and the trolley. Lot sizes vary widely, from narrow colony-era parcels to larger lots on the edges of the valley. Homes near the port and the freeways contend with idling traffic, while streets near the river valley have flood and environmental considerations.

What moves values in San Ysidro

Border proximity

Closeness to the port of entry brings commuter convenience along with traffic, idling vehicles and commercial activity, and buyers weigh both.

Freeway convergence

Interstate 5, Interstate 805 and State Route 905 meet here, and noise and air quality vary sharply by street.

Multiple units

Properties with two or more units call for income analysis and careful verification of which units are permitted.

Tijuana River Valley conditions

Flooding and cross-border sewage pollution affect areas near the valley and are noted where relevant.

Colony-era lots

Original Little Landers parcels and later subdivisions differ greatly in size and shape, limiting direct comparisons.

Appraisal assignments here

Immigration-related appraisals for affidavits of support are among the most common requests in San Ysidro, because families sponsoring relatives often use home equity to show assets. Estate and family transfer valuations arise from long-held homes and multi-unit properties. Divorce and bankruptcy assignments also occur, and bail bond appraisals sometimes involve San Ysidro properties offered as collateral, where a quick but well-supported value is needed.

San Ysidro appraisal questions

Can an appraisal be used for an immigration affidavit of support?

Yes. Brian Ward prepares appraisals that document market value as of a specific date so the sponsor can show equity as an asset. The report identifies comparables, condition and any additional units, giving reviewers a clear basis for the value and a credentialed appraiser's signature.

How are multi-unit properties in San Ysidro valued?

The appraisal uses sales of similar multi-unit properties and considers rental income where buyers rely on it. Permit status for each unit is verified with city records. Unpermitted units are reported separately and valued based on market evidence rather than counted as legal rental space.
