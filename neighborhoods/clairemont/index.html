Clairemont home appraisals

Postwar village within a city, split by canyons

Clairemont was one of the largest postwar subdivisions in San Diego, and thousands of its 1950s ranch houses are still standing on a mesa cut by Tecolote and San Clemente canyons. Similar floor plans hide large differences in condition, canyon exposure and remodeling, which is where a careful appraisal earns its keep.

The Clairemont market

Clairemont covers the broad mesa north of Linda Vista and east of Interstate 5, reaching up to State Route 52 and Marian Bear Memorial Park. North Clairemont, East Clairemont and Bay Ho each have their own commercial nodes, with Clairemont Town Square serving as the main shopping center. San Diego Mesa College sits on the southeastern edge near Kearny Mesa, and Clairemont and Madison high schools anchor the residential streets. Tecolote Canyon Natural Park winds through the middle, giving many homes open-space backyards. Demand has been strong among buyers priced out of the beach areas, which has driven a steady flow of remodels, second units and lot-line ADUs. The 30-foot height limit overlay keeps most rebuilding single-story or modest two-story.

How Clairemont took shape

Developers Carlos Tavares and Lou Burgener began Clairemont around 1950, naming it for Claire Tavares and marketing it as a village within a city with its own schools, parks and shopping. Construction moved quickly across the mesa through the 1950s and into the 1960s, absorbing aircraft and defense workers employed at nearby plants. Clairemont Town Square opened in the late 1950s. Later decades added condominiums and apartments on remaining parcels, and the 2021 Mid-Coast trolley extension placed stations along the western edge.

Homes and housing stock

The core stock is single-story ranch houses from the 1950s and early 1960s, often three bedrooms and one or two baths in roughly 1,000 to 1,400 square feet, on lots around 5,000 to 7,000 square feet. Post-and-beam and modest mid-century modern designs show up in some tracts. Many homes have been enlarged, and ADUs have become common since state law loosened restrictions. Canyon-rim lots are deeper or irregular. Townhome and condominium complexes from the 1970s and 1980s are scattered along Genesee Avenue, Clairemont Mesa Boulevard and the canyon edges, giving buyers attached options alongside the detached tracts.

What moves values in Clairemont

Canyon rim lots

Homes backing Tecolote or San Clemente canyon offer privacy and sometimes views but may carry slope, brush management and erosion considerations.

ADUs and conversions

Detached ADUs and garage conversions are widespread. Their permit status, rentability and quality are analyzed separately from the main house.

Height limit overlay

The 30-foot Clairemont Mesa height limit restricts second-story additions and affects what buyers can do with an original house.

Original condition

Many homes still have original systems and plumbing from the 1950s, so condition adjustments often exceed differences in size.

Airport and arterial noise

Areas near Balboa Avenue, Genesee Avenue and the approach paths for Montgomery-Gibbs Executive Airport experience more traffic and aircraft noise.

Appraisal assignments here

Clairemont's aging first- and second-generation owners make it one of the busiest areas for date-of-death, estate and trust appraisals in the city. Proposition 19 family transfers and gift valuations are common when children inherit or buy out siblings. Divorce assignments often involve homes with recent ADU construction, and pre-sale appraisals help heirs decide whether to sell as-is or renovate first.

Clairemont appraisal questions

Does an ADU add value to a Clairemont home?

Often, but not by its construction cost. Brian Ward looks at sales of comparable homes with permitted ADUs and, where helpful, at rental income. The added value depends on the unit's size, permit status, privacy and condition, and on how many buyers in that price range want a second unit.

Which Clairemont sales are used for an estate appraisal?

Sales closest to the date of death in the same part of Clairemont, with similar size, lot and condition, are preferred. For a retrospective value, Brian Ward uses sales closing near the effective date and adjusts for market change if older or slightly later sales are needed.
