Tierrasanta home appraisals

Planned canyon community on former Camp Elliott land

Tierrasanta was built from 1971 on land that was once a Marine Corps training range, and it is surrounded by canyon open space and Mission Trails Regional Park. Fire exposure, open-space views and a wide mix of associations make each property's position in the community central to its value.

The Tierrasanta market

Tierrasanta sits east of Interstate 15, south of State Route 52 and MCAS Miramar, with Mission Trails Regional Park on its eastern boundary and the San Diego River valley to the south. Greenbelts and about 818 acres of community open space thread between the residential villages, so many homes back onto canyons. Santo Road and Tierrasanta Boulevard carry most traffic, and the town center, recreation center and Canyon Hills High School form the civic core. Murphy Canyon, on the southwest side, holds a large military family housing complex. Detached homes and townhome associations are intermixed, and the community's relative isolation from through traffic is part of its appeal. Mission Trails access is a draw for buyers who hike and bike.

How Tierrasanta took shape

The land belonged to Mission San Diego de Alcala and later ranching interests. The military bought it in 1941 for Camp Elliott, used for Marine training until 1944, then by the Navy until 1946. The federal government sold the area to the city in 1961. Development began in 1971 and was largely complete by the early 1990s. Unexploded ordnance from the training years led to clearance work, and the Army Corps of Engineers still reviews the area under its formerly used defense sites program. The 2003 Cedar Fire destroyed homes here.

Homes and housing stock

Homes date mostly from the 1970s and 1980s. Detached houses include single-story and two-story tract designs, often 1,500 to 2,500 square feet, with three or four bedrooms and two-car garages. Townhome and condominium associations account for much of the housing supply, many with pools and shared greenbelt landscaping. Tierrasanta Norte in the northeast was built later than the original villages. Lots along canyon edges are larger or irregular and may have view or open-space frontage, while interior lots are more standard. Fire-hardening upgrades and roof replacements are common updates.

What moves values in Tierrasanta

Wildfire exposure

Many homes border canyon brush and Mission Trails, and fire hazard designation can affect insurance availability and cost.

Open-space frontage

Homes backing greenbelts or canyons often sell at a premium for privacy and views, balanced against brush management obligations.

HOA versus detached

Numerous associations with different dues, reserves and rules make attached-home comparables specific to each project.

Ordnance history

The former Camp Elliott ranges are part of local disclosure history. The appraisal notes publicly available information without speculating on any specific lot.

Freeway and military proximity

Homes near Interstate 15, State Route 52 or Miramar flight activity experience more noise than interior streets.

Appraisal assignments here

Tierrasanta's original 1970s and 1980s buyers are now aging, so estate, trust and date-of-death appraisals are common. Divorce assignments frequently involve townhomes and detached homes bought as family residences. Insurance-related valuation questions arise because of fire exposure, especially after carriers non-renew policies near the canyons. Pre-sale appraisals help owners decide how to price canyon-view homes relative to interior lots, and families with military ties sometimes need a value when a transfer order forces a quick decision to sell or rent.

Tierrasanta appraisal questions

Does wildfire risk affect Tierrasanta home values?

It can, mainly through insurance cost and availability. Brian Ward notes the property's fire hazard designation and its proximity to open space, then relies on sales of homes with similar exposure. When canyon homes and interior homes sell differently, the difference is measured from market data, not from an assumed discount.

How are Tierrasanta townhomes compared to each other?

Sales from the same association are preferred because dues, amenities and construction match. When none are available, the appraisal uses similar associations nearby and adjusts for differences in HOA costs, garage type, end-unit status and open-space views. Pending special assessments disclosed by the association are also considered.
