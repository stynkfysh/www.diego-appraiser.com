Allied Gardens home appraisals

Mid-1950s family tract between Mission Gorge and Lake Murray

Allied Gardens was built in the mid-1950s by a group of builders working together, and it still reads as one coherent tract of single-story homes. Small differences in lot position, updating and canyon frontage carry most of the value variation.

The Allied Gardens market

Allied Gardens occupies the hills east of Grantville and north of Interstate 8, part of the city's Navajo community planning area along with Grantville, Del Cerro and San Carlos. Waring Road and Zion Avenue are main routes, and the Allied Gardens Community Park and pool, Benjamin Branch Library and Lewis Middle School form a civic center that has changed little in decades. Mission Trails Regional Park and Lake Murray are a short drive away, and the Grantville trolley station connects to Mission Valley and SDSU. The neighborhood is overwhelmingly single-family, with few condo complexes, and buyer demand comes largely from families seeking a quiet tract near job centers. Homes on the higher streets may look across the valley, while lower streets are closer to Mission Gorge Road traffic.

How Allied Gardens took shape

Allied Contractors, a cooperative of builders, began the tract in the 1950s, and the name came from that partnership. Construction moved quickly across the hills above the San Diego River, producing thousands of similar ranch homes for postwar families. Schools, the library and the park followed within a few years. With almost no vacant land remaining after the 1960s, change has come through remodeling and, more recently, accessory dwelling units built in rear yards.

Homes and housing stock

The typical Allied Gardens home is a single-story ranch house of 1,000 to 1,500 square feet with three bedrooms, on a lot around 5,000 to 7,000 square feet. Exteriors mix stucco, wood siding and brick accents, with low-pitched roofs and attached garages. Many have been opened up, expanded or given second baths. Lots on hillside streets can be steep or terraced, while interior blocks are flat. ADUs have become more common since state law changed. Attached housing is limited, so detached-home comparables are usually plentiful within the tract itself, and original floor plans can often be matched model to model.

What moves values in Allied Gardens

Hillside versus flat lots

Upper streets offer valley views but steeper yards, while flat interior lots offer more usable space. Both are adjusted against similar lot types.

Remodel quality

Kitchen and bath updates vary from cosmetic to complete rebuilds, and condition is often the largest adjustment.

ADU potential and presence

Rear-yard units add rentable space on some lots, and their permit status matters to value.

Traffic exposure

Homes on Waring Road, Mission Gorge Road and Zion Avenue face more traffic than homes on cul-de-sacs.

Canyon edge brush

Lots bordering open slopes may carry brush management responsibilities and fire insurance considerations.

Appraisal assignments here

Allied Gardens has a large share of long-held homes, many owned by original buyers or their children, which makes estate, trust and date-of-death appraisals frequent. Proposition 19 family transfer valuations come up as homes pass between generations. Divorce appraisals are common in this family neighborhood, and pre-sale assignments help heirs understand how much an original-condition home is worth compared with remodeled ones nearby.

Allied Gardens appraisal questions

Are Allied Gardens homes appraised using sales from Del Cerro or San Carlos?

Sales from Allied Gardens itself are preferred because the tract is large and consistent. Brian Ward expands to Del Cerro, San Carlos or Grantville only when needed and adjusts for location, view, lot size and home design differences between those neighborhoods.

How much does an original-condition home differ from a remodeled one?

The difference can be significant, but it is measured from local sales rather than from a formula. The appraisal compares the subject with homes in similar condition when possible and documents the condition with photographs and notes from the inspection, including original systems such as galvanized plumbing or older electrical panels.
