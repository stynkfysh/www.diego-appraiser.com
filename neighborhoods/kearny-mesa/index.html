Kearny Mesa home appraisals

Employment and dining hub with a small residential base

Kearny Mesa is primarily a business district, home to the Convoy District, major hospitals and Montgomery-Gibbs Executive Airport. The few homes and the newer apartments and condos rising under its updated plan require comparables chosen with unusual care.

The Kearny Mesa market

Kearny Mesa spans the mesa between Clairemont and Serra Mesa, north of Mission Valley and south of Marine Corps Air Station Miramar. Convoy Street has become the city's best-known corridor for Asian restaurants and markets, while Sharp Memorial Hospital, Rady Children's Hospital and Kaiser Permanente's San Diego Medical Center draw thousands of workers. Montgomery-Gibbs Executive Airport occupies the center of the mesa. Residential property is limited and scattered, from older condo complexes to newer multifamily buildings encouraged by the 2020 community plan update, which opened large areas to mixed-use housing. Because sales of homes inside Kearny Mesa are thin, appraisals often draw on nearby Clairemont, Serra Mesa or Linda Vista while accounting for the commercial setting.

How Kearny Mesa took shape

The mesa takes its name from General Stephen W. Kearny, and Camp Kearny occupied the area during World War I. Montgomery Field, named for glider pioneer John J. Montgomery, became a general aviation airport, and after World War II the mesa developed as an industrial and aerospace district with Convair's large plant. Hospitals and offices followed. The Convoy corridor grew into a dining destination from the 1980s onward, and the 2020 community plan update shifted policy toward housing near jobs.

Homes and housing stock

Owner-occupied housing is a small share of land use. Existing residential includes condominium and townhome complexes from the 1970s through the 2000s near the edges of the business district, along with apartment communities. New construction since the 2020 plan update is mostly mid-rise multifamily, some with ground-floor retail. Detached houses are rare and generally sit near the boundaries with Clairemont and Serra Mesa. Unit sizes run from studios to three-bedroom townhomes, and buildings differ in parking, amenities and exposure to industrial neighbors and aircraft. Some older complexes were built as rentals and later converted to condominiums, which can leave thin reserves and mixed owner-occupancy.

What moves values in Kearny Mesa

Thin residential sales

Few homes sell inside Kearny Mesa, so the comparable search often extends to adjacent neighborhoods, with location adjustments for the commercial setting.

Aircraft noise

Montgomery-Gibbs operations and airport land use compatibility rules affect residential desirability and redevelopment near the field.

Industrial adjacency

Homes and condos next to industrial or auto uses can sell at a discount to similar units on quieter streets.

New versus older condos

Recent multifamily construction and 1980s complexes appeal to different buyers and are not interchangeable comparables.

Zoning changes

The 2020 plan update raised allowable density in many areas, which can affect land value for older properties.

Appraisal assignments here

Residential assignments in Kearny Mesa are less frequent than in neighboring tracts but often more complex. Estate and divorce appraisals typically involve condominiums or townhomes where comparables must be drawn carefully. Litigation and expert witness work can arise from disputes over properties near commercial uses. Pre-purchase appraisals help buyers evaluate new units where developer pricing is the main reference point. Because so many owners here are investors, rental condos also show up in trust administrations and partnership buyouts.

Kearny Mesa appraisal questions

How does Brian Ward find comparables for a Kearny Mesa condo?

Sales from the same complex come first. If there are none, he looks at similar complexes in Kearny Mesa and then in nearby Clairemont, Serra Mesa or Linda Vista, adjusting for age, amenities, HOA costs and location factors such as aircraft and traffic noise. The report explains why each sale outside the complex was chosen.

Does the Convoy District affect nearby home values?

Proximity to dining and jobs is attractive to some buyers, while traffic and commercial activity deter others. The appraisal measures how the market responds using sales of properties with similar proximity rather than assuming a premium or penalty. Parking pressure on residential streets near popular restaurants is noted when it applies.
