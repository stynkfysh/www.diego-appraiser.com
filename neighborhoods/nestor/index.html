Nestor home appraisals

Former Japanese American farm district near the river valley

Nestor began as a farming community, and some of its old agricultural parcels still sit among later tract homes near the Tijuana River Valley and Imperial Beach. Lot size, environmental conditions in the valley and the quality of owner-built improvements all bear heavily on value.

The Nestor market

Nestor is in the Otay Mesa-Nestor community planning area, bordered by Palm City and Otay Mesa West to the east, Egger Highlands to the north, San Ysidro to the southeast and the Tijuana River Valley to the south. Coronado Avenue, Saturn Boulevard, Hollister Street and Tocayo Avenue are its main roads. South Bay Salt Works operates at the northern edge along the bay, and the South Bay Drive-In on Coronado Avenue is the last drive-in theater still standing in San Diego. Southwest High School sits on Hollister Street, and Mount Olivet Cemetery dates to 1899. Housing ranges from older homes on oversized lots to postwar tracts and apartments. Cross-border sewage pollution in the Tijuana River Valley has been a long-running regional problem, and buyers near the valley take it into account.

How Nestor took shape

The neighborhood takes its name from Nestor A. Young, a state assemblyman in the mid-1880s who later served as San Diego harbormaster. It began as an agricultural district farmed in part by Japanese American families, whose operations were disrupted by wartime incarceration. San Diego annexed Nestor in 1957 along with other South San Diego land. Farms were subdivided into residential tracts in the postwar decades, though some larger lots and semi-rural parcels still survive near the river valley.

Homes and housing stock

The stock runs from older cottages and ranch houses on large lots to 1950s through 1970s tract homes on standard lots. A typical detached home is single-story, 1,000 to 1,600 square feet, with three bedrooms. Larger parcels may carry RV parking, workshops, second dwellings or room for animals, which sets them apart from tract comparables. Apartments and townhomes cluster near the main roads and the trolley stations at Palm Avenue and Iris Avenue. Additions, garage conversions and second units are common, with permit histories that vary widely. Low-lying lots near the valley may be in mapped flood zones.

What moves values in Nestor

Oversized lots

Some parcels have far more land than tract lots, supporting extra parking, outbuildings or ADUs, and need comparables with similar land area.

Tijuana River Valley conditions

Sewage-related pollution and odor in the valley are documented regional concerns, and proximity to the valley is weighed against sales data.

Flood exposure

Low-lying areas near the river valley may fall in FEMA flood zones, affecting insurance costs.

Owner-built improvements

Additions and second units of varying quality and permit status are common and must be verified on site.

Freeway and trolley access

Interstate 5 and the Blue Line add convenience but also noise for nearby homes.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate and family transfer appraisals are common in Nestor because many homes have been held for decades, sometimes on parcels larger than anything recently sold nearby. Divorce and bankruptcy valuations also arise, often involving second units. Immigration-related appraisals for affidavits of support are frequent in South San Diego. Pre-sale appraisals help heirs price properties with large lots or unpermitted structures before deciding whether to clean up permits first.

Nestor appraisal questions

Do larger lots in Nestor add value?

Usually, but the amount depends on how the extra land can be used. Brian Ward compares sales of similar-sized parcels where possible and considers street access, zoning, flood status and whether the land could support an ADU, RV parking or a lot split. Unusable rear land is not valued the same as a flat, accessible yard.

Does Tijuana River Valley pollution affect home values in Nestor?

The appraisal notes publicly reported conditions as of the effective date and checks whether nearby sales show a measurable effect, for example by comparing homes closer to and farther from the valley. An adjustment is made only when the market data supports one, and the reasoning is explained in the report.
