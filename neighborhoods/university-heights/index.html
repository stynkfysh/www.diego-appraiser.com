University Heights home appraisals

Streetcar-era blocks on the mesa above Mission Valley

University Heights sits on the mesa north of Hillcrest and west of North Park, with Park Boulevard and Adams Avenue as its main streets. Canyon-rim lots, small-lot bungalows and many apartment buildings sit close together, so values can change sharply over a short distance.

The University Heights market

University Heights is mostly older houses and small apartment buildings on a grid that ends at steep canyons dropping to Mission Valley. Park Boulevard has restaurants and shops, and Adams Avenue begins its long commercial stretch here before heading east. Trolley Barn Park, on the site of the old streetcar barn, is the neighborhood's central gathering place. The Vermont Street Bridge connects pedestrians with Hillcrest. Houses on the north rim have views across Mission Valley, while interior blocks have small lots, many with added units. Apartment buildings from the 1960s and 1970s are common, and some have been converted to condominiums. Appraisals need to separate rim homes from interior homes and treat permitted and unpermitted second units carefully.

How University Heights took shape

The University Heights subdivision was laid out in the late 1880s on land set aside for a planned college, which gave the neighborhood its name. The State Normal School opened at Park Boulevard and El Cajon Boulevard in 1898. Streetcar service along Park Boulevard and Adams Avenue brought commuters, and Mission Cliff Gardens operated on the canyon rim as a park and garden in the early twentieth century. Bungalow construction peaked in the 1910s and 1920s, and apartments replaced some houses after World War II.

Homes and housing stock

Craftsman bungalows and Spanish Revival houses from the 1910s through the 1930s make up most of the single-family stock, on lots that are often small and narrow. Many lots have a second home, a converted garage or a newer ADU. Bungalow courts and small multi-unit buildings appear throughout, and 1960s and 1970s apartment buildings line parts of Park Boulevard and the side streets. Some of those buildings became condominiums. Houses on the north rim have larger lots, steeper slopes and views. Newer townhomes and small condo projects fill some corner lots.

What moves values in University Heights

Canyon rim views

Homes on the north rim with views across Mission Valley sell at a premium over interior homes. View width and the condition of the slope are both considered.

Second units and ADUs

Many lots have more than one unit, some permitted and some not. Legal status affects both value and how the property should be compared.

Small-lot bungalows

Interior homes are often under 1,200 square feet on narrow lots. Size adjustments are based on sales of similar small homes rather than larger North Park or Kensington houses.

Condo conversions

Older apartment buildings converted to condos have different maintenance and soundproofing than newer buildings. They are compared with similar conversions.

Corridor exposure

Homes on Park Boulevard or Adams Avenue get traffic and bus noise, and sales are checked against quieter streets for any discount.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate and trust appraisals come up frequently because many bungalows near Trolley Barn Park have been held by one family for decades, often with an added rear unit nobody permitted. Divorce cases often involve a house with a rental unit, which requires valuing the income alongside the house. Pre-sale appraisals help owners price homes with unpermitted additions before buyers' inspectors find them. Family transfers and partnership buyouts of small multi-unit properties along Park Boulevard are also common.

University Heights appraisal questions

How are unpermitted units handled in a University Heights appraisal?

Brian Ward reports the legal use and does not count an unpermitted unit as a legal unit. He may note the market's response to such units when sales show buyers paying for them, along with the cost or risk of legalizing them. The report makes the treatment clear so the value can be relied on in a legal or tax setting.

Are rim homes compared with homes on interior blocks?

Only with adjustments. Rim homes have views and sometimes larger lots, but also slopes and possible fire or geotechnical concerns. The best comparables are other rim sales in University Heights, Normal Heights or Hillcrest, with interior sales used only to support adjustments.
