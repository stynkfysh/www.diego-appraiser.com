Talmadge home appraisals

1920s tract named for silent film stars

Talmadge sits east of Kensington, bordered by canyons to the north and west and El Cajon Boulevard to the south. Its original 1920s homes, historic gates and mix of single-family and multifamily buildings make careful comparable selection important.

The Talmadge market

Talmadge is a residential neighborhood known for its 1920s subdivisions, decorative gates at street entrances and a traffic circle at its center. The neighborhood was named for silent film stars Norma, Constance and Natalie Talmadge. Canyons border the north and west, and El Cajon Boulevard forms the southern edge with commercial buildings from the 1930s and 1940s. Housing includes Spanish Revival homes, bungalows, Cape Cod and Normandy-style homes, and apartment buildings built in the 1960s through 1980s, especially near the southern edge. Appraisers must account for historic character, canyon-rim views, multifamily buildings and the difference between quiet interior streets and homes near El Cajon Boulevard. The Talmadge Gates Historical District is part of the neighborhood.

How Talmadge took shape

Developers Roy and Guy Lichty founded Talmadge in the mid-1920s, and a dedication ceremony in January 1926 drew large crowds and Hollywood guests. The neighborhood was named after the Talmadge sisters, whose family connections helped promote the project. Decorative gates were installed at street entrances in the late 1920s. El Cajon Boulevard became part of U.S. Route 80 in 1929, bringing commercial growth. From the 1960s through the 1980s, some single-family homes were replaced by apartments.

Homes and housing stock

Housing includes Spanish Revival homes, California bungalows, Cape Cod and Normandy-style cottages from the late 1920s and 1930s, and smaller cottages from the World War II years. Lot sizes vary, with canyon-rim lots on the northern edge often larger or steeper than those on the interior grid. Apartment buildings from the 1960s through the 1980s are common near El Cajon Boulevard and along the southern edge, where some original houses were replaced. Condos and townhomes exist, mainly in converted or newer buildings. Most single-family homes are modest in size, often under 1,600 square feet, with detached garages.

What moves values in Talmadge

Historic gates district

The Talmadge Gates Historical District adds character and may affect alterations. Historic status is considered in comparables.

Canyon-rim views

Rim homes may have views and privacy. Views are adjusted with similar canyon sales.

Multifamily mix

Apartment buildings near El Cajon Boulevard affect nearby home values and may be comparables for income properties. Income analysis is used when needed.

El Cajon Boulevard exposure

Homes near the boulevard get more traffic and noise. Paired sales show any discount.

Architectural style

Spanish Revival and Normandy-style homes can sell at a premium. Style and condition are adjusted carefully.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate and trust appraisals are common for original 1920s homes still owned by long-time residents. Divorce and partnership cases may involve apartment buildings near El Cajon Boulevard, which call for income analysis alongside comparable sales. Pre-sale appraisals help owners price period homes inside the gates district. Small income properties may also need valuation for property tax appeals or for estate planning before a transfer to the next generation.

Talmadge appraisal questions

Does being in the Talmadge Gates Historical District affect value?

It can affect both desirability and what owners can change. Brian Ward considers whether buyers have paid more for homes inside the district than for similar homes outside it, and notes any historic restrictions or Mills Act contracts that affect property taxes and alterations.

How are Talmadge apartment buildings valued?

Apartment buildings are valued using comparable sales of similar small multifamily properties and, when appropriate, an income analysis. Rents, expenses, vacancy and unit mix are reviewed, along with any rent-control or tenant-protection rules that apply, and the two approaches are reconciled in the report.
