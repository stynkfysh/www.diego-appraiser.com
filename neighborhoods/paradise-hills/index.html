Paradise Hills home appraisals

Low-density hillside homes south of Paradise Valley

Paradise Hills is a hilly neighborhood at the southern edge of San Diego, bordering National City, with single-family homes spread across slopes above Paradise Valley. Lot shape, slope and views from higher streets shape value here.

The Paradise Hills market

Paradise Hills lies south of Paradise Valley Road, north of State Route 54, east of Rachael Avenue and the National City line, and west of Dusk Drive and Potomac Street. It is part of the Skyline-Paradise Hills community planning area. Paradise Valley runs east-west through the community, and Paradise Creek flows toward San Diego Bay. Housing is predominantly low-density single-family homes on hillside lots. The Paradise Hills Recreation Center, Penn Athletic Field and a skate park serve residents. The name is sometimes applied loosely to surrounding areas, so Brian Ward confirms the exact location before selecting comparables. Slope, lot usability and views from higher streets are the main factors that separate otherwise similar homes.

How Paradise Hills took shape

Paradise Hills developed mainly after World War II as tract housing spread south from National City and east from the bay. Its location close to the Navy facilities along the bay made it attractive to military families, and many homes were bought by Navy personnel. From the Vietnam era onward, Filipino-American families settled here and in neighboring Bay Terraces and Skyline in large numbers. The neighborhood has remained primarily residential, with commercial uses concentrated along a few streets.

Homes and housing stock

Most homes are single-family houses from the postwar decades, including ranch and stucco tract designs, typically between 1,100 and 1,800 square feet with attached garages. Lots vary, with some on steep slopes or canyon edges where usable yard area is limited. Many homes have been updated, expanded with additions or fitted with converted garages. Condominiums and apartments are limited. Homes on higher streets may have views toward the bay, the valley or the mountains. Because the housing is fairly uniform in age, condition and permitted additions often explain most differences between sales.

Appraisal assignments here

Because many houses were bought by Navy families in the postwar decades, the original owners are now aging, and trust, estate and conservatorship-related appraisals are frequent. Parent-to-child transfers follow. Divorce appraisals often involve homes with additions or converted garages. Property tax appeals arise when assessed values outpace what nearby sales support, and pre-sale appraisals help heirs decide whether to sell or keep a home as a rental.

Paradise Hills appraisal questions

Is Paradise Hills the same as Bay Terraces?

No. They are separate neighborhoods within the same Skyline-Paradise Hills planning area and share the 92139 ZIP code, which causes frequent mix-ups in listings and records. Brian Ward confirms the property's exact location and selects comparables from the correct neighborhood, since housing age and street patterns differ between the two.

Do views add value in Paradise Hills?

They can. Higher streets may look across the valley toward the bay, and buyers often pay for that. Brian Ward compares the property with sales that have similar views, preferably in Paradise Hills itself, and supports any adjustment with local data rather than a rule of thumb.
