Point Loma home appraisals

Navy-ringed peninsula of bayfront estates and hillside views

Point Loma rises from the harbor to a ridge with views of the bay, downtown and the Pacific, and its neighborhoods range from historic La Playa estates to postwar ranch houses in Fleetridge. View direction, elevation and lot size do most of the work in setting value here.

The Point Loma market

Point Loma occupies the peninsula that shelters San Diego Bay, with military land, Cabrillo National Monument and Fort Rosecrans National Cemetery covering its southern tip. Residential areas climb from the bayfront in La Playa and Roseville up through Fleetridge and the Wooded Area to the ridge along Catalina Boulevard, then fall toward the ocean side and Sunset Cliffs. Shelter Island holds marinas and hotels, and Rosecrans Street carries commercial activity near the waterfront. Homes on the bay side look toward downtown, Coronado and the harbor, while ocean-side homes look west. Lot sizes are larger than in most coastal San Diego neighborhoods, especially in the Wooded Area. Because no two view lots are alike, Brian Ward documents the view during inspection and compares it with what the comparables actually offered.

How Point Loma took shape

Juan Rodriguez Cabrillo landed near Ballast Point in 1542. Louis Rose laid out Roseville along the bay in 1869, and La Playa served as an early landing for hide and whaling operations. From 1897 the Theosophical Society under Katherine Tingley built its Lomaland community on the ocean side, a campus later occupied by Point Loma Nazarene University. Military installations expanded through both world wars. Fleetridge and the Wooded Area were subdivided mainly in the 1940s through 1960s, filling the hillsides with custom and tract homes.

Homes and housing stock

La Playa and parts of Roseville hold older estates, Spanish Revival homes and newer custom houses on bayfront and near-bay lots. Fleetridge and Point Loma Heights are dominated by 1940s through 1960s ranch and mid-century homes, many enlarged with second stories. The Wooded Area has larger, irregular lots under mature pines and eucalyptus, with custom homes from the 1950s through today. Condominiums are concentrated near Shelter Island and Rosecrans Street. Detached homes commonly range from about 1,500 to over 4,000 square feet, and the 30-foot coastal height limit restricts how far owners can build up for views.

What moves values in Point Loma

Bay versus ocean views

Bay and city-light views and ocean sunset views appeal to different buyers. Comparables are matched by view type and breadth, not just by the word view.

Coastal height limit

Prop D limits buildings to 30 feet, so existing view-capturing height can be difficult to replicate. Older homes with grandfathered features may carry an advantage.

Lot size and topography

Wooded Area and hillside lots vary widely in usable area and slope. Steep or irregular lots require separate land adjustments.

Military and flight activity

Navy aircraft, ship traffic and proximity to base gates affect some streets. Airport approach noise reaches the northern edge near Loma Portal.

Historic and older homes

La Playa and Roseville include homes with historic significance that may qualify for Mills Act contracts or face review before alteration.

Appraisal assignments here

Estate and trust appraisals are common because many Point Loma homes have been owned by Navy retirees and long-term families since the 1950s and 1960s. Retrospective date-of-death valuations require careful handling of view premiums at the time of death. Divorce appraisals frequently involve high-value view homes where small differences in view or condition move results. Property tax appeals and gift valuations also arise on bayfront estates.

Point Loma appraisal questions

How does Brian Ward value the view on a Point Loma home?

He photographs and describes the view from the main living areas during inspection, noting direction, breadth and obstructions. He then selects comparables with similar views and uses paired sales, where available, to support any view adjustment. The report explains the reasoning so a reader can see how the view was weighed.

Are Wooded Area lots valued differently from Fleetridge lots?

Yes. Wooded Area lots tend to be larger and more irregular, with mature trees and more privacy, while Fleetridge has more uniform tract lots. Those differences affect land value, so comparables are drawn from the same area where possible and adjusted for usable lot size.
